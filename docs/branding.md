# Branding Ideas

## Concept: The "Gold Standard" (Classic Speakeasy)

This design focuses on the high-class, exclusive feeling of a hidden card club. It uses art deco geometric structure, rich metals, and symmetry.

### The Visuals

* **The Logo:** A circular badge designed to look like polished brass and deep blue enamel. The central monogram ("RR") is stylized art deco, seamlessly interwoven with a prominent run of 4 cards. This signifies that "Rum Runner" *is* cards. The background subtly integrates small rum barrels. The typography is bold, geometric, and clean (ideal for web scalability).
* **The Icon:** The icon is just the stylized monogram. It fits perfectly inside a favicon or app icon square, remaining legible even when tiny.

## Other Branding Suggestions for "Rum Runner"

Your new name provides a goldmine for immersive branding that goes far beyond just a logo. Here is how you can use the theme to make your Rummy Club feel like a destination:

**1. A Rich, Low-Light Color Palette**
Ditch standard "web white." Your platform should feel cozy and clandestine.

* **Primary:** Very dark charcoals, deep blues, or rich, warm browns (like aged leather or wood).
* **Secondary:** Polished brass/gold, or a creamy, aged-parchment off-white for text (easier on the eyes than pure white).

**2. Thematic Player Ranks (The Ladder)**
Instead of "Bronze, Silver, Gold," let players climb the ranks of the 1920s underground:

* *Level 1:* **Associate**
* *Level 5:* **Bootlegger**
* *Level 10:* **Runner** (They have cleared the lowest ranks)
* *Level 20:* **Distiller**
* *Level 30:* **Smuggler**
* *Level 40:* **Enforcer**
* *Level 50:* **Capo**
* *Level 75:* **Kingpin**
* *Max Level:* **The Boss** or **The Don**

**3. In-Game Terminology (The "Flavor")**
Use thematic language in your UI to deepen the immersion.

* Instead of "Join a Tournament," use **"Enter the High-Stakes Room."**
* Instead of "Player Profiles," use **"The Dossier."**
* A players-only chat/forum could be **"The Speakeasy Forum"** or **"The Backroom."**
* If you have a digital currency, call it **"Script"** or **"Credits."**

**4. Table Aesthetics**
The actual game screen should reinforce the club atmosphere.

* The card table background should default to **dark green felt** or, better yet, a **rich, polished mahogany wood texture.**
* The default card backs should match your chosen logo concept (e.g., the intricate gold RR monogram).

## Sonic Branding: The Sound of the Club

The audio is the "Gold Standard" heard instead of seen. Close your eyes at the table and the room should still read as Rum Runner: card stock whispering across felt, a knuckle on a mahogany door, chips and glassware clicking somewhere behind you, and — only when something is worth celebrating — a warm phrase of muted brass from the house band. The club is hushed by nature. Every sound is short, low, and physical; nothing in this room ever bleeps.

**1. The Sonic Identity**
If the visual palette is dark charcoal, aged leather, and polished brass under low light, the sonic palette is its acoustic twin: **warm, muted, and materials-driven.** Sounds come from things a 1920s card room actually contains — paper, felt, wood, brass, glass. Digital-sounding tones, bright arcade chirps, and synthetic sparkle are the audio equivalent of "web white": they break the spell instantly. When in doubt, quieter and shorter is more on-brand than louder and longer.

**2. The Material Vocabulary**
Each visual brand pillar has a sound. Use this rubric whenever sourcing or designing a new cue:

| Brand pillar | Sound material | Reserved for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green felt & card stock | Soft slides, ruffles, flips — air and paper, no pitch | Routine card handling: drawing, discarding, dealing, diving the pile |
| Mahogany & aged wood | Low, dry knocks and thumps | Decisive placements: a meld snapped down, the knock at the door |
| Polished brass | Warm, mellow chime and horn tones — felt-hammered, never glassy-bright | Attention and triumph: your turn, hands won, the game itself |
| Barware & chips | Tiny clinks and pops | Social moments: chat, a new player at the table |
| The basement hum | Low, quiet, rounded tones | Trouble: errors, drops, forfeits — the lights flickering, never an alarm |

**3. Direction by Cue Category**
The existing cue inventory falls into five families. Direction for each:

* **Routine card actions** (`draw-stock`, `draw-discard`, `pile-dive`, `discard`, `deal`, `meld`, `layoff`): quiet, brief, felt-and-paper physical. The most frequent sounds must be the most self-effacing — a discard happens every turn and should register without ever being noticed. `meld` may land with a firmer wood-backed snap (it is a declaration); `layoff` is its lighter cousin; `pile-dive` is a multi-card ruffle; `deal` is the shuffle-ruffle that says "the hand begins."
* **The knock** (`knock`): the brand's gift. Literal knuckles on a wooden door — the speakeasy's own password. Keep it dry, real, and doubled. Never replace it with a tone.
* **Attention cues** (`your-turn`): noticeable but never shrill — the bartender tapping a glass once, not a doorbell. Warm brass character, polite volume, one strike.
* **The celebration ladder** (`hand-over` → `go-out` → `gin`, with `game-over` above all): escalating brass warmth. Winning a hand earns a short two-note phrase; going out earns a rising figure; gin earns the full flourish; game over is the band actually finishing a phrase — the biggest moment in the product, yet still a club combo, not a stadium fanfare. `undercut` is the wry exception: a descending, minor-tinged sting — schadenfreude with a raised eyebrow. House motif rules: **wins ascend, losses descend.**
* **Negative & system cues** (`error`, `hand-cancelled`, `disconnect`, `reconnect`, `forfeit`): muted, low, and brief. An error is a quiet low buzz — the dealer shaking his head, not a game-show buzzer. `hand-cancelled` stays neutral (a whoosh, no verdict). `disconnect` descends, `reconnect` ascends as its exact mirror — trouble and relief share a motif. `forfeit` is somber and falling. Nothing in this family may ever feel punishing; players who misclick are guests, not offenders.
* **Social cues** (`chat`, `player-joined`): glassware. A tiny pop or clink for another player's message (never one's own), a soft ding as someone new steps through the door.

**4. Mix Rules of the House**

* **Three loudness tiers.** Routine card actions sit lowest; attention and social cues sit in the middle; outcome moments sit on top — and even the top tier is restrained. If a sound competes with conversation, it is too loud.
* **Actions are blinks, outcomes are moments.** Card-handling cues live well under half a second; chimes and stings under a second or so; only `game-over` may breathe longer, and never past a couple of seconds.
* **Silence is the default state.** No background music, no looping ambience, no idle chatter. The hush *is* the low-light brand — a sound only ever means something just happened. (A future ambient layer would be a deliberate brand decision, not a filler.)
* **Restraint under fire.** Rapid play — a multi-meld turn in the 500 Rummy game variation — must sound like fluent card handling, not clatter. Repeats of the same cue overlap sparingly and never stack into noise.
* **One sound per moment.** When several things resolve at once (a gin that ends the hand), play the single most meaningful cue, never a pile-up.

**5. Do / Don't**

* **Do** record or source real materials: card stock, wood, felt, brass, glass.
* **Do** keep everything warm, dry, and close — the sound of a small room, not a hall.
* **Do** reuse motifs in mirrors and ladders (ascend/descend, small win/big win) so the ear learns the language.
* **Don't** use casino slot-machine jingles, coin cascades, or ka-chings — this is a private club, not a casino floor.
* **Don't** use cartoon boings, whistles, or comedy stingers.
* **Don't** use harsh digital buzzers, sirens, or alarm tones — not even for errors.
* **Don't** use bright synthetic chimes, arcade bleeps, or "notification" sounds that could belong to a phone OS.
* **Don't** add click sounds to every button — UI chrome stays silent; only the game speaks.

**6. Technical Delivery Specs**

* **Format: WAV, mono, 16-bit PCM.** Today's generated set is 22 050 Hz; sourced replacements target mono 32 kHz. WAV stays the delivery format deliberately — compressed formats add decoder padding that delays exactly the moment a card sound must land.
* **One file per cue, named for the cue** (`meld.wav`, `your-turn.wav`, …) — filenames are a contract with the client.
* **Consistent perceived loudness within each tier** (loudness-normalized, with quieter targets for routine actions than for alerts and outcomes), soft peaks, and short edge fades so nothing clicks.
* **Total budget ≈ 1.5 MB** across the full set — the club loads once and quickly.
* **Licensing: CC0 only** for sourced assets, with provenance recorded per file. The brand cannot rest on sounds it does not own outright.
